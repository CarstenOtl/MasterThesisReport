\chapter{Experiments in Simulation}\label{ch:sim}%

This chapter reports the simulation experiments. Section~\ref{sec:sim-setup} fixes the experimental setup. Section~\ref{sec:sim-teacher} reports the training of the privileged teachers. Section~\ref{sec:sim-student} reports the main student ablations in simulation, Section~\ref{sec:sim-design} the design ablations of the student, and Section~\ref{sec:sim-online} compares offline with online distillation. Section~\ref{sec:sim-inventory} inventories every ablation that the code base supports and marks which of them have results. Numbers that are not yet available are marked \tbd{} and listed in the draft to-do file.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:sim-setup}

All simulation experiments use the system of Section~\ref{sec:system} in Isaac~Sim~5.1 with physics at 120\,Hz and control at 15\,Hz, on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX~5090. Teachers are trained with 512 parallel environments for 200 epochs, which takes about ten hours per teacher; the student is trained offline for 300 epochs in about 30 minutes. The student data set is the one of Section~\ref{sec:dataset}: 20 successful ten-cycle demonstrations of the M24 hexagonal nut collected from 32 environments with 30 random arm impulses each, 19\,486 frames in total, under lighting and texture randomization of the dome light, the tabletop, the nut and bolt textures, the floor texture, and the image background in the style of DextrAH-RGB~\cite{Singh2025}. Figure~\ref{fig:sim-scenes} shows the parallel training scenes and the randomized student views. Unless stated otherwise, students use the Cartesian action contract with $H=8$, sixteen Euler steps, a temporal-ensemble decay of 0.5, EMA weights, and the DP3-style encoder; evaluation follows Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} with ten trials of ten cycles on the M24 nut in a nominal scene without physical domain randomization.

\section{Student Ablations in Simulation}\label{sec:sim-student}

The main ablation study addresses the four questions of Section~\ref{sec:research-questions}: whether one student trained on multi-cycle data beats a single-cycle policy that is invoked repeatedly (Q1), what the point-cloud encoder contributes (Q2), and whether the flow-matching head beats deterministic regression with recurrent and Transformer backbones (Q3); the zero-shot transfer of Q4 is evaluated on hardware in Chapter~\ref{ch:real}. Five students are trained on the simulated dataset of Section~\ref{sec:dataset} and evaluated under matched budgets; each ablation changes exactly one component of the full method.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] \emph{Single-cycle student.} The student is trained on demonstrations of one cycle and is invoked ten times in a trial; its temporal context is reset after every cycle.
  \item[(b)] \emph{ResNet visual encoder.} The point-cloud encoder is replaced by the one-branch image-space encoder of Section~\ref{sec:student}, an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 stage-2 trunk that fuses RGB, log depth, a validity mask, and the calibrated per-pixel XYZ coordinates into the same 64-D feature. Policy, objective, data, and budget are unchanged.
  \item[(c)] \emph{Point-cloud encoder with LSTM regression.} The flow-matching Transformer is replaced by the DextrAH-RGB-style recurrent backbone of Section~\ref{sec:baselines}, a layer-normalized 512-unit LSTM followed by a 512--512--256 ELU MLP, trained by deterministic action regression on the same data.
  \item[(d)] \emph{Point-cloud encoder with Transformer regression.} The deterministic ACT-style Transformer of Section~\ref{sec:baselines} predicts the eight-step chunk from eight learned action queries.
  \item[(e)] \emph{Full method.} One flow-matching student with the point-cloud encoder is trained on the complete multi-cycle simulated demonstrations.
\end{enumerate}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation-sim} reports the results on the M24 hexagonal nut. The full method (e) completes all ten cycles in every trial, with a cyclic tracking error of $-1.77\pm1.02\degree$ and a ten-cycle error of $-17.7\pm3.23\degree$: the student lags the $60\degree$ target by less than two degrees per cycle, and the lag accumulates without drift in its spread over the trial. The four ablations isolate the components that make this possible. The single-cycle student (a) removes the axial advance of the nut from the data: in a single-cycle demonstration the nut is always grasped at its reset height, whereas over ten cycles it rises by one sixth of a pitch per cycle, and the multi-cycle student has to read this height from the point cloud (Q1). The ResNet student (b) keeps everything but the representation (Q2), and the two regression heads (c) and (d) keep the data and, for (d), the backbone, the chunk length, and the temporal ensembling, so that the difference to (e) isolates the generative objective (Q3). Their results on the simulated dataset are \tbd.

\section{Design Ablations of the Student}\label{sec:sim-design}

The ablations of Section~\ref{sec:sim-student} compare the final student against alternatives at the level of whole components. The design of the student itself was fixed by a sequence of controlled single-rollout ablations on six-cycle sequential-threading data, which this section reports because they document why each component is present. All runs in this section use the privileged 6-D nut-pose observation or the RGB-D observation as stated, 20 impulse-augmented demonstrations, 300 epochs, the Cartesian contract, $H=8$, and the student-owned cyclic evaluation with a six-cycle requirement at a fixed seed; they are matched single rollouts, not success rates.

\subsection{Action Contract}\label{sec:sim-contract}

The Cartesian contract of Section~\ref{sec:action-contract} is supported by a direct comparison on pickup demonstrations with the privileged 6-D observation. Materialized in the native Cartesian contract, the demonstrations produced a flow student with 10/10 grasps and lifts, 5/10 terminal successes on the handoff-only task, and 8/10 qualified handoffs, 7/10 engagement entries, and 2/10 held engagements on the stricter engagement task. Materialized in the 17-D joint-PD contract, the same demonstrations produced a student that fitted its labels equally well offline (training flow loss 0.03, teacher-fit chunk RMSE 0.040, correlation 0.998) and grasped and lifted in every trial, but reached the handoff waypoint in none; terminal-hold padding and a residual variant that predicts arm-position residuals left this unchanged. Imitating the bounded, bolt-relative action that the teacher itself produced is therefore what turns a good offline fit into closed-loop task progress, and all later students use the Cartesian contract. \subsection{Progress and Phase Conditioning}\label{sec:sim-conditioning}

On six-cycle sequential-threading demonstrations with policy-only impulses, a state-conditioned flow student completed four of the six cycles. With impulses in every phase segment and the global trajectory progress $p_t$ added to the condition, the student completed all six required cycles in 735 control ticks with zero transition failures; its release times, however, drifted increasingly behind the teacher, by $+8.6$\,s at the sixth release, and the fifth cycle took twice as long as the others. Adding the three-way process-phase encoding reduced the completion time to 609 ticks, equalized the cycle durations to 6.7 to 7.1\,s, reduced the release-time errors against the teacher to within $\pm0.5$\,s, reduced the controlled-joint RMSE against the nominal trajectory from $11.5\degree$ to $4.7\degree$ and the TCP position RMSE from 75\,mm to 28\,mm, and reduced the final cumulative nut-turn error from $-60\degree$ to $-8\degree$. The offline teacher-fit of the phase-conditioned checkpoint was marginally worse than that of the progress-only checkpoint (first-action RMSE 0.0046 against 0.0044), so the closed-loop improvement is not explained by a better fit; the phase encoding acts as a synchronization signal that keeps the student's internal clock aligned with physical events when the cycle duration deviates from nominal. Both conditions have been enabled by default since. Table~\ref{tab:conditioning} collects the numbers.

\subsection{Vision Encoder and Pretraining}\label{sec:sim-vision}

The first RGB-D student used the two-branch cross-attention encoder with an auxiliary nut-position head and completed all six cycles in 617 ticks, matching the privileged student; a recollection with explicit success markers and retraining reproduced the result in 627 ticks. The retained DP3 encoder is pretrained on rendered sweeps of a free-moving nut from the calibrated camera, which reaches a held-out position error of 5.9\,mm and a hexagon-symmetry-aware rotation error of $46\degree$; fine-tuning on hybrid-teacher images with phase-balanced sampling reaches 1.9\,mm and $3.8\degree$ on held-out teacher episodes, and 2.8\,mm and $6.6\degree$ on images from a student rollout. The ten-cycle DP3 student of Table~\ref{tab:ablation-sim} was initialized from this sweep-pretrained encoder with all encoder parameters trainable; it completed all ten cycles in 1072 ticks with a maximum turn of $60.3\degree$ and a mean absolute retained-turn error of $3.1\degree$. Its release times lag those of its source demonstration by 2.7\,s on average and by 7.4\,s at the tenth cycle, a timing drift that does not affect the completion of the cycles. A closed-loop comparison of the DP3, Adapt3R-style, and ResNet encoders under identical data and budget beyond ablation (b) is \tbd{} (Table~\ref{tab:encoders}).

\section{Offline versus Online Distillation}\label{sec:sim-online}

The offline flow student is compared with online DAgger using the recurrent LSTM student on cyclic threading from privileged 6-D observations under the Cartesian contract, with the hybrid teacher as the label source and a ten-cycle cap per episode. The intervention schedule determines which part of the cycle the online student learns. Under full teacher control ($\beta=1$) it learns the threading turn; under full student control ($\beta=0$) with teacher labels it learns the turn and the complete release-and-return transition; and with an intervention probability that decays linearly from full teacher to full student control it learns both and completes two uninterrupted student-only turn-release-return cycles. The offline flow student completes all six cycles of the conditioning study with privileged observations and all ten cycles from RGB-D. Table~\ref{tab:online} summarizes the comparison.

The evaluation ladder of Section~\ref{sec:ladder} explains where the offline student's advantage comes from. Table~\ref{tab:ladder-results} places several students on its first rung next to their closed-loop outcome. Every listed checkpoint reproduces its training labels with first-action RMSE below 0.04 in normalized action units and correlation above 0.99, and the online LSTM students likewise fit their labels and execute them correctly in teacher-forced mode. The offline fit is therefore a necessary diagnostic and not a predictor of task success; the closed-loop column is decided by the contract, the conditioning, and the data. The retained student combines the three properties that the closed loop rewards: the Cartesian contract, the progress and phase conditions, and demonstrations that contain the teacher's recoveries from disturbances in every phase. Together with chunked prediction and temporal ensembling, these let it recover from its own deviations under its own observations, which is the regime in which the online recurrent students fall behind.
