\chapter{Experiments in Simulation}\label{ch:sim}%

This chapter tests the method of Chapter~\ref{ch:method} in simulation, where every component can be varied under matched conditions. It answers RQ1 and RQ2 of Section~\ref{sec:research-questions}; RQ3, the zero-shot transfer, needs the physical system and is evaluated in Chapter~\ref{ch:real}. Section~\ref{sec:sim-setup} fixes the setup that all experiments share. Section~\ref{sec:sim-teacher} shows that the privileged teacher and the hybrid rollout produce reliable multi-cycle demonstrations, the precondition for every student. Section~\ref{sec:sim-student} reports the main ablations that answer RQ1 and RQ2. Section~\ref{sec:sim-design} reports the design ablations that fixed the action contract, the conditioning, and the encoder of the student, and Section~\ref{sec:sim-online} compares offline with online distillation. Section~\ref{sec:sim-inventory} lists every ablation that the code base supports and marks which of them have results, and Section~\ref{sec:sim-summary} summarizes the chapter. Numbers that are not yet available are marked \tbd.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:sim-setup}

All simulation experiments run the system of Section~\ref{sec:system} in Isaac~Sim~5.1 with physics at 120\,Hz and control at 15\,Hz on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX~5090. Each teacher is trained with 512 parallel environments for 200 epochs, which takes about ten hours. The student is trained offline for 300 epochs in about 30 minutes on the dataset of Section~\ref{sec:dataset}: 20 successful ten-cycle demonstrations of the M24 hexagonal nut, collected from 32 environments with 30 random arm impulses each, 19\,486 frames in total. The demonstrations are rendered under lighting and texture randomization of the dome light, the tabletop, the nut and bolt textures, the floor texture, and the image background in the style of DextrAH-RGB~\cite{Singh2025}. Figure~\ref{fig:sim-scenes} shows the parallel training scenes and the randomized student views. Unless stated otherwise, students use the Cartesian action contract with $H=8$, sixteen Euler steps, a temporal-ensemble decay of 0.5, \acs{EMA} weights, and the DP3-style encoder. Evaluation follows Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}: ten trials of ten cycles on the M24 nut in a nominal scene without physical domain randomization.

\section{Teacher Training}\label{sec:sim-teacher}

\label{sec:sim-experts}
Every demonstration comes from the hybrid teacher, so the teacher has to turn the nut reliably and hand over cleanly to the scripted release and return. The teacher result is also the experimental test of difficulty~(b) of Section~\ref{sec:problem-setup}: whether a learned policy acquires the pinch grasp and keeps it under the turning load through the commanded hand and arm degrees of freedom. With the simplified thread model and the three-stage reward of Section~\ref{sec:teacher}, the threading expert learns the single $60\degree$ turn from the engaged nut. On an earlier humanoid-arm platform, the recipe with the $\pm30\degree$ initial-yaw curriculum reached a population success rate of 83.6\,\% at the end of training, with a peak of 90.0\,\%. On the final platform, the retained M24 teacher, trained for 200 epochs with the curriculum and the axial-height randomization, completed ten consecutive cycles in all 32 hybrid-teacher candidate environments without a single transition failure. This is the property that matters for demonstration generation. The single-turn success rate of this teacher at the final curriculum level is \tbd.

\section{Student Ablations in Simulation}\label{sec:sim-student}

The main ablation study answers RQ1 and RQ2 of Section~\ref{sec:research-questions}. Five students are trained on the simulated dataset of Section~\ref{sec:dataset} and evaluated under matched budgets, and each ablation changes exactly one component of the full method. Ablation (a) addresses RQ1: it tests whether one student trained on multi-cycle data outperforms a single-cycle policy that is invoked repeatedly. Ablations (b) to (d) address RQ2: (b) replaces the point-cloud encoder with an image-space encoder, and (c) and (d) replace the flow-matching head with deterministic regression on a recurrent and on a Transformer backbone. RQ3 is evaluated on hardware in Chapter~\ref{ch:real}.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] \emph{Single-cycle student.} The student is trained on demonstrations of one cycle and is invoked ten times in a trial; its temporal context is reset after every cycle.
  \item[(b)] \emph{ResNet visual encoder.} The point-cloud encoder is replaced by the one-branch image-space encoder of Section~\ref{sec:student}, an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 stage-2 trunk that fuses RGB, log depth, a validity mask, and the calibrated per-pixel XYZ coordinates into the same 64-D feature. Policy, objective, data, and budget are unchanged.
  \item[(c)] \emph{Point-cloud encoder with \ac{LSTM} regression.} The flow-matching Transformer is replaced by the DextrAH-RGB-style recurrent backbone of Section~\ref{sec:baselines}, a layer-normalized 512-unit \ac{LSTM} followed by a 512--512--256 ELU \acs{MLP}, trained by deterministic action regression on the same data.
  \item[(d)] \emph{Point-cloud encoder with Transformer regression.} The deterministic ACT-style Transformer of Section~\ref{sec:baselines} predicts the eight-step chunk from eight learned action queries.
  \item[(e)] \emph{Full method.} One flow-matching student with the point-cloud encoder is trained on the complete multi-cycle simulated demonstrations.
\end{enumerate}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation-sim} reports the results on the M24 hexagonal nut. The full method (e) completes all ten cycles in every trial, with a cyclic tracking error of $-1.77\pm1.02\degree$ and a ten-cycle error of $-17.7\pm3.23\degree$. The student thus lags the $60\degree$ target by less than two degrees per cycle, and the lag accumulates over the trial without its spread drifting. Each ablation removes one property on which this result may depend. The single-cycle student (a) never sees the axial advance of the nut: in a single-cycle demonstration the nut is always grasped at its reset height, whereas over ten cycles it rises by one sixth of a pitch per cycle, and the multi-cycle student has to read this height from the point cloud (RQ1). The ResNet student (b) keeps everything but the representation. The regression students (c) and (d) keep the data and, for (d), also the backbone, the chunk length, and the temporal ensembling, so that their difference to (e) isolates the generative objective (RQ2). Their results on the simulated dataset are \tbd.

\section{Design Ablations of the Student}\label{sec:sim-design}

The main ablations compare the final student with alternatives at the level of whole components. The design of the student itself was fixed earlier by a sequence of controlled ablations, which this section reports because they explain why each component is present. The action-contract comparison uses pickup demonstrations; the remaining design ablations use six-cycle sequential-threading data. Except where a subsection varies them, all runs use the privileged 6-D nut-pose observation or the \acs{RGBD} observation as stated, 20 impulse-augmented demonstrations, 300 epochs, the Cartesian contract, $H=8$, and the student-owned cyclic evaluation with a six-cycle requirement at a fixed seed. The results are matched single rollouts, not success rates.

\subsection{Action Contract}\label{sec:sim-contract}

The student imitates the teacher's bounded, bolt-relative Cartesian action because a joint-space contract fails in closed loop despite an equally good offline fit. Table~\ref{tab:contract} compares the two contracts on pickup demonstrations with the privileged 6-D observation. Materialized in the native Cartesian contract of Section~\ref{sec:action-contract}, the demonstrations produced a flow student with 10/10 grasps and lifts, 5/10 terminal successes on the handoff-only task, and 8/10 qualified handoffs, 7/10 engagement entries, and 2/10 held engagements on the stricter engagement task. Materialized in the 17-D joint-PD contract, the same demonstrations produced a student that fitted its labels equally well offline (training flow loss 0.03, teacher-fit chunk \acs{RMSE} 0.040, correlation 0.998) and grasped and lifted in every trial, but reached the handoff waypoint in none. Neither terminal-hold padding nor a residual variant that predicts arm-position residuals reached the handoff waypoint either. A good offline fit therefore turns into closed-loop task progress only when the student imitates the action that the teacher itself produced, and all later students use the Cartesian contract.

\subsection{Progress and Phase Conditioning}\label{sec:sim-conditioning}

The global progress scalar lets the student finish all cycles, and the process-phase encoding keeps it synchronized with the physical cycle. On six-cycle sequential-threading demonstrations with policy-only impulses, a state-conditioned flow student completed four of the six cycles. With impulses in every phase segment and the global trajectory progress $p_t$ added to the condition, the student completed all six required cycles in 735 control ticks with zero transition failures. Its release times, however, drifted increasingly behind the teacher, by $+8.6$\,s at the sixth release, and the fifth cycle took twice as long as the others. Adding the three-way process-phase encoding reduced the completion time to 609 ticks and equalized the cycle durations to 6.7 to 7.1\,s. It also reduced the release-time errors against the teacher to within $\pm0.5$\,s, the controlled-joint \acs{RMSE} against the nominal trajectory from $11.5\degree$ to $4.7\degree$, the \acs{TCP} position \acs{RMSE} from 75\,mm to 28\,mm, and the final cumulative nut-turn error from $-60\degree$ to $-8\degree$. The offline teacher fit of the phase-conditioned checkpoint was marginally worse than that of the progress-only checkpoint (first-action \acs{RMSE} 0.0046 against 0.0044), so a better fit does not explain the closed-loop improvement. The phase encoding acts instead as a synchronization signal that keeps the student's internal clock aligned with physical events when the cycle duration deviates from nominal. All later students use both conditions. Table~\ref{tab:conditioning} collects the numbers.

\subsection{Vision Encoder and Pretraining}\label{sec:sim-vision}

The visual students match the privileged student, and the retained \ac{DP3} encoder owes its accuracy to pretraining on rendered nut sweeps. The first \acs{RGBD} student used the two-branch cross-attention encoder with an auxiliary nut-position head and completed all six cycles in 617 ticks, matching the privileged student; a recollection with explicit success markers and retraining reproduced the result in 627 ticks. The retained \ac{DP3} encoder is pretrained on rendered sweeps of a free-moving nut from the calibrated camera and reaches a held-out position error of 5.9\,mm and a hexagon-symmetry-aware rotation error of $46\degree$. Fine-tuning on hybrid-teacher images with phase-balanced sampling reduces these errors to 1.9\,mm and $3.8\degree$ on held-out teacher episodes, and to 2.8\,mm and $6.6\degree$ on images from a student rollout. The ten-cycle \ac{DP3} student of Table~\ref{tab:ablation-sim} was initialized from this sweep-pretrained encoder with all encoder parameters trainable. It completed all ten cycles in 1072 ticks with a maximum turn of $60.3\degree$ and a mean absolute retained-turn error of $3.1\degree$. Its release times lag those of its source demonstration by 2.7\,s on average and by 7.4\,s at the tenth cycle, a timing drift that does not affect the completion of the cycles. \section{Offline versus Online Distillation}\label{sec:sim-online}

Offline flow distillation produces students that complete every required cycle, whereas the online \ac{DAgger} students complete at most two. The comparison uses online \ac{DAgger} with the recurrent \ac{LSTM} student on cyclic threading from privileged 6-D observations under the Cartesian contract, with the hybrid teacher as the label source and a ten-cycle cap per episode. The intervention schedule determines which part of the cycle the online student learns. Under full teacher control ($\beta=1$) it learns the threading turn. Under full student control ($\beta=0$) with teacher labels it learns the turn and the complete release-and-return transition. With an intervention probability that decays linearly from full teacher to full student control, it learns both and completes two uninterrupted student-only turn-release-return cycles. The offline flow student completes all six cycles of the conditioning study with privileged observations and all ten cycles from \acs{RGBD}. Table~\ref{tab:online} summarizes the comparison.

The evaluation ladder of Section~\ref{sec:ladder} shows that the offline fit does not explain this difference. Table~\ref{tab:ladder-results} places several offline students on its first rung next to their closed-loop outcome. Every listed checkpoint reproduces its training labels with first-action \acs{RMSE} below 0.04 in normalized action units and correlation above 0.99, and the online \ac{LSTM} students likewise fit their labels and execute them correctly in teacher-forced mode. The offline fit is therefore a necessary diagnostic and not a predictor of task success; the closed-loop outcome follows the contract, the conditioning, and the data. The retained student combines the three properties that the closed loop rewards: the Cartesian contract, the progress and phase conditions, and demonstrations that contain the teacher's recoveries from disturbances in every phase. Together with chunked prediction and temporal ensembling, these properties plausibly let it recover from its own deviations under its own observations, which is the regime in which the online recurrent students fall behind.

\section{Summary}\label{sec:sim-summary}

In simulation, the hybrid teacher produces error-free ten-cycle demonstrations, and the full method trained on them completes all ten cycles of the M24 task in every trial with a cyclic error of $-1.77\pm1.02\degree$. The design ablations attribute this result to the Cartesian contract, the progress and phase conditions, and demonstrations with recoveries from disturbances in every phase, and they show that the offline fit alone does not predict it. Whether the multi-cycle data (RQ1) and the point-cloud encoder and flow-matching head (RQ2) are each necessary will be settled by the reruns of ablations (a) to (d) on the simulated dataset. Simulation cannot show whether the student bridges the gap in contact and perception that the simplified thread model and the rendered images leave open. Chapter~\ref{ch:real} therefore deploys the same student, trained on simulated data only, on the physical system to answer RQ3.
