\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{ch:conclusion}%

This chapter summarizes what the thesis achieved, answers the research questions, and outlines the work that remains between the demonstrated skill and robotic assembly with threaded joints.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Threaded joints can be released again and their threads are standardized~\cite{Budynas2020}, yet fastening a nut is still largely left to people and specialized tools. This thesis asked how a dexterous robot can learn to thread a nut. Threading poses four difficulties at once. Engaged threads are too contact-rich to simulate faithfully at the scale that \ac{RL} needs, and a cheaper contact model can be exploited. A dexterous hand has a high-dimensional action space and many ways to solve the task. Threading is a long-horizon cyclic task in which errors compound across cycles and phase transitions. The skill has to be learned in simulation and transferred to hardware across the gaps in contact, perception, and actuation that the simulation opens.

The thesis answers these difficulties by decomposing the task during data generation and unifying it at deployment. During data generation the cycle is decomposed into a turning skill and scripted transitions. A privileged asymmetric actor--critic learns the pinch-grasp turn in a simplified thread simulation in which axial progress follows from rotation. Scripted release and return chain the turns into a hybrid teacher that produces complete multi-cycle demonstrations with physically gated phase boundaries. At deployment the behavior is unified again. One visuomotor student, distilled from the demonstrations by conditional flow matching, executes the whole cycle from an \acs{RGBD} camera and proprioception. It was trained on simulated data only, and a calibrated camera model, a metric point cloud cropped to the workspace, visual domain randomization, and a shared Cartesian action path are designed to carry it to hardware.

In simulation, the distilled student completes all ten cycles in every trial on the M24 hexagonal nut, with a mean cyclic tracking error of $-1.77\degree$ and a ten-cycle error of $-17.7\degree$. A single-cycle student invoked ten times, the comparison that tests the principle (RQ1), completes $5.5\pm0.8$ cycles and never reaches ten. On hardware, the student transfers zero-shot and again completes all ten cycles in every trial, with a mean cyclic tracking error of $-4.21\degree$.

The three research questions are answered as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[RQ1] One student distilled from complete multi-cycle demonstrations sustains all ten cycles in simulation and on hardware. A single-cycle policy invoked repeatedly completes $5.5\pm0.8$ cycles in simulation and $5.2\pm1.2$ cycles on hardware, because it does not follow the axial advance of the nut.
  \item[RQ2] The flow-matching student with a point-cloud encoder is the only tested design that sustains the cyclic task. An image-space encoder completes $2.3\pm0.9$ cycles in simulation and $1.1\pm0.9$ on hardware, and its feature carries about twice the orientation error of the point-cloud feature. Deterministic \ac{LSTM} regression diverges before the first cycle, and deterministic Transformer regression completes $1.4\pm0.8$ cycles.
  \item[RQ3] The student is trained on simulated data only and deployed without real data or training on the robot. It completes all ten cycles in every trial on the M24, M30, and M36 hexagonal nuts, with mean cyclic tracking errors between $-3.70\degree$ and $-5.52\degree$. It transfers to the held-out octagonal and round profiles at all three sizes and fails on the rectangular profile. In stress trials without a cycle limit, it sustains 15 to 22 cycles.
\end{enumerate}

With respect to the motivation of Section~\ref{sec:motivation}, a dexterous hand can thread a nut in simulation over ten consecutive cycles with a learned policy that is trained without real data. The policy re-grasps the nut in every cycle from camera data at the angle and height at which the previous cycle left it. It does so zero-shot on the physical system as well, on hexagonal nuts of three sizes and on unseen octagonal and round profiles (RQ3). It cannot yet start from a loose nut, tighten the joint, choose a different grasp when the resistance grows, or match the speed of a dedicated tool (Section~\ref{sec:disc-assembly}). Section~\ref{sec:future-work} addresses these gaps.

\section{Future Work}\label{sec:future-work}

Autonomous engagement of a loose nut requires grasping the nut, transporting it to the bolt, and chaining these steps with threading. Seating the nut on the bolt is a separate skill with a narrow entry configuration. It will most likely require fingertip-force and tactile feedback, for which the fingertip-force and joint-torque observations of teacher and student are a starting point.

Tightening to a preload needs a thread model whose resistance rises when the nut seats, a grasp that transmits the resulting torque, and a policy that senses when the joint is tight. The same force observations can serve as inputs for such a policy.

The demonstrations start at the nominal nut height and cover only the 5.0\,mm of axial travel of ten cycles. A curriculum that widens this range toward the full usable thread length would extend the horizon of the teacher and of the student with it. The global progress clock should be replaced at the same time, because it binds the student to a nominal trial duration. Inferring the phase from the observation history inside the policy would remove that dependency.

For the transfer, the randomization ranges of the simulated camera should be fitted to measured statistics of the physical D415, including depth failures, exposure, motion blur, and camera and control latency. The depth-corruption model that the encoder pretraining already provides can then be applied to the student's training data as well.

Training on more than one face topology would extend the re-grasp strategy to profiles that a hexagon does not cover, and other grasp topologies than the thumb--index pinch would let the hand adapt its grasp to the resistance of the thread.

The distillation study can be completed with the online flow-matching student, which is implemented and remains to be evaluated. Collecting multi-step teacher recovery fragments from states that the student visits would give it on-policy labels without violating the constraint that a chunk label must be produced by the teacher alone. The pending sweeps over the action horizon, the number of integration steps, and the temporal-ensemble decay, and the closed-loop comparison of the point-cloud encoder with the Adapt3R-style encoder, are further ablations that Chapter~\ref{ch:experiments} does not cover.
