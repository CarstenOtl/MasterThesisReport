\chapter{Discussion}\label{ch:discussion}%

This chapter interprets the results of Chapters~\ref{ch:sim} and~\ref{ch:real} with respect to the four questions of Section~\ref{sec:research-questions}, relates the offline and online distillation variants, discusses the action and observation contracts, returns to the questions of the exposé, and states the limitations of the work.

\section{Interpretation of the Ablation Study}\label{sec:disc-ablations}

\paragraph{Unified multi-cycle student (Q1).}
The full method completes all ten cycles in every simulated trial with a lag of less than two degrees per cycle. What makes this possible is visible in the content of the data. In a single-cycle demonstration the nut height at grasp is always the reset height, so a student trained on it never has to read the height from the point cloud and adjust its approach; in a multi-cycle demonstration the height changes by one sixth of a pitch per cycle, and the student learns to track it. The global progress scalar supports the multi-cycle student, but it is not the source of the axial information, which comes from the coverage of the axial state in the demonstrations. The single-cycle ablation (a), which receives the same progress scalar, isolates this effect; its result is \tbd. The design principle that the study tests is that the unification of the complete cyclic behavior in one dataset, rather than a per-cycle controller invoked repeatedly, yields a policy that sustains consecutive cycles.

\paragraph{Point-cloud encoder (Q2).}
Re-grasping requires the orientation of the nut faces relative to the fingers; a representation that encodes position but not orientation would let the fingers close on the right spot with the wrong face alignment. The point-cloud encoder consumes the metric geometry of nut, bolt, and hand directly, and the sweep-pretrained encoder, fine-tuned on hybrid-teacher images, recovers the nut pose to 1.9\,mm and $3.8\degree$ on held-out teacher episodes. It reaches this with about 60\,000 parameters and no pretrained image backbone. The same geometric property is what the zero-shot transfer relies on: inside the crop box the point cloud is identical in both domains up to depth noise and calibration error, whereas an image-space encoder sees the full appearance gap between rendered and real images. Random point sampling adds an augmentation that the image encoder does not receive. The closed-loop ResNet ablation (b) and the pose probe quantify the difference; their results are \tbd.

\paragraph{Flow matching versus deterministic regression (Q3).}
The chunked Transformer regression head (d) shares the backbone, the chunk length, and the temporal ensembling of the flow student, so the difference between (d) and (e) isolates the generative objective. Two mechanisms favor the flow head. The first is multimodality of the labels: the dataset contains, for near-identical observations, the teacher's nominal action and the teacher's recovery after an impulse; a squared-error head converges to their average, an action that neither demonstration contains, whereas the flow head samples one of them. The second is the structure of the prediction: the flow head predicts a velocity toward the data manifold from a noisy chunk, which is a denoising problem that is easier to fit than a direct regression from observation to chunk and which, through the sixteen integration steps, refines the chunk jointly over all eight steps with full attention among them. The ablation results of (c) and (d) on the simulated dataset are \tbd.

\paragraph{Zero-shot sim-to-real transfer (Q4).}
The student is trained on simulated data only, and its transfer rests on four properties of the pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:sim2real}). The calibrated camera model makes the simulated and real point clouds geometrically comparable; the crop box removes the background, which is where rendered and real images differ most; lighting, texture, and background randomization together with image-space color augmentation cover the remaining appearance variation of the points that enter the encoder; and the shared Cartesian action path executes the student's commands through a controller that reproduces the simulated control law, under which the teacher was trained with randomized gains and noisy pose inputs. The approach requires no real-world data collection and no training on the robot. The hardware results that test it are \tbd.

\section{Offline versus Online Distillation}\label{sec:disc-online}

The thesis set out, in the exposé and in the code base, to distill the teacher online with DAgger, and it ends with an offline flow student. The comparison in Section~\ref{sec:sim-online} shows why. The online recurrent students learn the turn, the transition, or both, depending on who controls the environment, and the evaluation ladder shows that they fit their labels and execute them correctly under teacher-forced observations. The offline flow student sustains ten cycles under its own observations because its training data contain explicit recoveries from disturbances in every phase, because its chunked generative prediction with temporal ensembling smooths single-step errors, and because the progress and phase conditions keep it synchronized with the physical cycle. DAgger's advantage, labels on student-visited states, is obtained here in a different form by the impulses, which move the teacher into off-nominal states and record its recovery. The online flow variant with $\beta=1$ obtains labels on teacher-visited states only and therefore reduces to online collection of the same data, and the chunked-label constraint rules out the mixture schedules that the one-step recurrent student uses; an online variant on student-visited states would need multi-step teacher recovery fragments collected from student states. The offline dataset has a further practical advantage: it is rendered once under visual randomization and shared unchanged by every student and ablation, so that the comparisons of Chapter~\ref{ch:sim} differ only in the component under test, and one student trains in about thirty minutes.

\section{Action and Observation Contracts}\label{sec:disc-contracts}

The project implemented two action contracts and settled on the Cartesian one. The joint-PD contract was motivated by deployment convenience and by the expectation that joint references are easier for a controller to track than Cartesian targets, which they are: recorded joint references replayed at the physics rate reproduce the teacher motion with sub-millimeter accuracy. As a learning target, however, the Cartesian contract is the stronger choice. Converting the teacher's bounded Cartesian action into seventeen physical-unit references at the physics rate and then asking the student to emit one such reference per 15\,Hz step introduces a hold error that the teacher never produced, mixes units with scale differences of three orders of magnitude in one regression target, and removes the bolt-relative structure of the action that lets the student generalize across nut heights. The Cartesian contract keeps the student in the teacher's own action space and executes it through the unchanged controller, including the action smoothing that the teacher was trained with, and with the same demonstrations it turns an equally good offline fit into closed-loop progress. The conclusion is that a student should imitate the action the teacher produced, in the units the teacher produced it, and let the controller do the conversion it was designed for.

The observation contract follows the same logic. The student receives the previous filtered action and the controlled joints, which is what the teacher's history contains, and the two low-dimensional conditions that the demonstration provides without privileged state. The global progress scalar assumes a nominally timed trial, and the ten-cycle simulation run shows that the student's release times drift behind those of its source demonstration by several seconds by the tenth cycle without affecting completion. The process-phase encoding keeps the student synchronized within each cycle, and in the ten-cycle run the drift does not lead to a failed transition. For trials that run much longer than the demonstrations, inferring the phase from the observation history inside the policy, rather than from an external signal, is the natural next step and is discussed in Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion}.

\section{Answers to the Questions of the Exposé}\label{sec:disc-expose}

The first question of the exposé asked whether vanilla PPO can learn a force-aware threading policy with a dexterous hand--arm system in a privileged, high-fidelity simulation without constraining the thread interaction. The work answers it with a sharper formulation. PPO learns a robust threading turn once the thread is constrained kinematically and the learned task is one turn from an engaged nut: trained under initial-yaw, axial-height, and controller randomization, the retained teacher, chained into the hybrid teacher, completes ten consecutive cycles in all 32 candidate environments. The kinematic constraint is what makes the learning problem well posed, because it ties axial progress to rotation, so that the progress reward can only be earned by turning; the hybrid teacher then extends the single turn to arbitrarily many cycles. Force awareness, the second part of the question, is not needed for the turn: the actor of the retained teacher observes no force channel. Hardware-equivalent force observations are implemented for teacher and student and remain to be evaluated.

The second question asked whether the privileged teacher can be distilled into a vision-based student with the localization accuracy that sub-millimeter thread interaction requires. The answer is that the student does not need to localize the nut to sub-millimeter accuracy, because the engagement of the nut on the bolt and the compliance of the impedance controller absorb lateral error; what the student needs is the orientation of the faces for the re-grasp and the height of the nut for the approach, and the point-cloud representation provides both with a precision that completes ten cycles in simulation. The exposé's expectation that explicit depth would be required is confirmed, and the deciding factor is the geometric structure of the representation rather than image resolution or stereo.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:disc-limitations}

The scope of the work defines its limitations. The nut starts engaged on the bolt, so the full task from table to tightened nut is outside the demonstrated behavior. The demonstrated behavior is a pinch-grasp re-grasp strategy learned on the face topology of a hexagon, and the student has no mechanism to discover a different grasp for a profile that requires one. The axial range of the demonstrations is bounded by the 20\,mm initial-height randomization of the teacher, and the global progress clock is defined over the nominal trial length. The evaluation uses ten trials per condition and reports means and standard deviations without confidence intervals, and several design ablations in Chapter~\ref{ch:sim} are matched single rollouts at one seed, which establish the direction of an effect but not its variance. The teacher's thread model is a kinematic simplification, and the randomization ranges that are meant to cover the resulting sim-to-real gap have not yet been fitted to measured statistics of the physical sensor. Finally, the camera calibration is measured but not deployment-validated, and the dependence of the student on calibration error has not been quantified.
