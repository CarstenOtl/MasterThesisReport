\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{ch:conclusion}%

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This thesis learned long-horizon cyclic nut threading with a dexterous hand--arm system in simulation and prepared its zero-shot transfer to hardware. The method separates two things that a monolithic policy would have to solve at once. During data generation the cycle is decomposed into a turning skill, learned by a privileged asymmetric actor--critic in a simplified thread simulation in which axial progress follows from rotation, and release-and-return transitions, which are scripted; chained, they form a hybrid teacher that produces complete multi-cycle demonstrations with physically gated phase boundaries. At deployment the behavior is unified again: one visuomotor student, distilled from the demonstrations by conditional flow matching, executes the whole cycle from an RGB-D camera and proprioception.

The student is trained on simulated data only. A camera model calibrated against the physical cell, a metric point cloud cropped to the workspace, visual domain randomization of lighting, textures, and background, and an action path that executes the teacher's own Cartesian action through a matched impedance controller are the properties that carry it across the sim-to-real gap without real-world data collection or training on the robot.

The student itself was built up component by component, and the thesis documents why each component is present. The point-cloud encoder preserves the face orientation that re-grasping needs and is invariant to appearance up to depth noise. The flow-matching head represents the heterogeneous labels of nominal teacher actions and disturbance recoveries instead of averaging them. Training on multi-cycle data teaches the student to follow the axial advance of the nut. The bounded, bolt-relative Cartesian action contract of the teacher is the imitation target that turns a good offline fit into closed-loop progress. The global progress scalar and the process-phase encoding keep the student synchronized with the physical cycle, and demonstrations with recoveries in every phase let it correct its own deviations. A three-rung evaluation ladder separated supervised fit, command execution, and closed-loop recovery, and it showed that the offline flow student's advantage over online DAgger with recurrent regression heads lies in recovery under its own observations.

In simulation the distilled student completes all ten cycles in every trial on the M24 hexagonal nut, with a mean cyclic tracking error of $-1.77\degree$ and a ten-cycle error of $-17.7\degree$. The zero-shot hardware results on the M24, M30, and M36 nuts and on held-out profiles are \tbd. Taken together, the results support the principle that organized the work: for long-horizon, contact-rich dexterous tasks, structured generation of demonstrations followed by their distillation into one policy is an effective route to a deployable skill.

\section{Future Work}\label{sec:future-work}

The first extension concerns the transfer. The randomization ranges of the simulated camera should be fitted to measured statistics of the physical D415, including depth failures, exposure, motion blur, and camera and control latency, and the depth-corruption model that the encoder pretraining already provides can then be applied to the student's training data as well.

The second extension is a longer horizon. The axial range of the demonstrations is bounded by the 20\,mm initial-height randomization of the teacher; a curriculum that widens this range toward the full usable thread length would extend the horizon of the teacher and of the student with it. The global progress clock should be replaced at the same time, because it binds the student to a nominal trial duration; inferring the phase from the observation history inside the policy would remove that dependency.

The third extension is autonomous engagement of a loose nut. The pickup expert already grasps, lifts, and transports the nut; seating it on the bolt is a separate skill with a narrow entry configuration. It will most likely require fingertip-force and tactile feedback, and the hardware-equivalent fingertip-force and joint-torque observation channels that are implemented for teacher and student are the starting point.

The fourth extension concerns the distillation. The online flow-matching student is implemented and remains to be evaluated; collecting multi-step teacher recovery fragments from states that the student visits would give it on-policy labels without violating the constraint that a chunk label must be produced by the teacher alone. The pending sweeps over the action horizon, the number of integration steps, and the temporal-ensemble decay, and the closed-loop comparison of the point-cloud encoder with the Adapt3R-style encoder, would complete the design study. Finally, training on more than one face topology would extend the re-grasp strategy to profiles that a hexagon does not cover.
