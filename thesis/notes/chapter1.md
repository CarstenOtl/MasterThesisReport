\section{Motivation}\label{sec:motivation}

A world built for human hands is full of parts and tools that a robot can only use if it can hold them, reorient them, and manipulate them. Multi-fingered dexterous hands promise this versatility, and learning-based control has recently made (in-hand manipulation with such hands) practical in simulation and on hardware~\cite{BicchiKumar2000,Weinberg2024,Chen2021,Handa2024}. Mechanical assembly is among the most demanding applications of this capability, because a firm grasp of one part has to be maintained while a fine, contact-rich motion joins it to another part.


Fastening a nut onto a bolt is an everyday example and one of the hardest to automate. The parts mate through sub-millimeter thread geometry, progress is governed by friction and contact forces that are difficult to sense, and the fastener must stay in the grasp while it is turned. \section{Problem Setup}\label{sec:problem-setup}

This thesis considers the task of threading a hexagonal nut onto a vertically mounted bolt with a seven-degree-of-freedom Franka Research~3 (FR3) arm that carries an Inspire RH56DFX five-fingered hand. The nut starts engaged on the bolt by a fraction of a turn. One threading cycle consists of approaching the nut, establishing a pinch grasp between thumb and index finger on two opposing faces, rotating the nut clockwise by a target angle of $60\degree$ while it advances along the thread, holding the target, releasing the nut, and returning the hand to a re-grasp pose. A trial requires ten such cycles in sequence. The deployed policy has to execute them from an aligned RGB-D camera and the robot's proprioception alone, without privileged object state or scripted controllers.

The skill is learned in simulation and transferred to the physical robot. A faithful thread simulation is expensive, because engaged threads produce thousands of simultaneous contacts~\cite{Narang2022}, and the reinforcement-learning stage of this work therefore uses a simplified thread model that couples a passive revolute joint to a position-controlled prismatic joint while keeping the finger--nut contact forces. The simplification opens a sim-to-real gap in contact and perception that the transfer method has to close.

Two properties of the task shape the method, which Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes. The task is long-horizon and cyclic, so a policy has to sustain consecutive cycles and is sensitive to errors at phase boundaries; this favors generating the training signal phase by phase, where each phase is easy to specify. The task is also only partially observable at deployment, because the nut pose that a simulator exposes to a privileged policy has to be inferred from camera images by the deployed policy; this favors a teacher--student scheme in which a privileged teacher is distilled into a sensor-based student~\cite{Singh2025,Oertel2026}. The method of this thesis follows from these two observations: demonstrations are generated by a hybrid teacher that decomposes the cycle into a learned turning skill and scripted transitions, a single visuomotor student is distilled from these demonstrations in simulation, and the student is transferred to the physical robot zero-shot, where it executes the complete cyclic task.

\section{Research Questions}\label{sec:research-questions}

**The exposé of this thesis posed two research questions** --> irrelevant for this thesis don't mention it. The first asked whether a force-aware policy that threads a nut onto a bolt with a dexterous hand--arm system can be learned with proximal policy optimization (PPO) in a privileged simulation without constraining the thread interaction. The second asked whether such a privileged teacher can be distilled into a vision-based student that estimates the object pose accurately enough for sub-millimeter thread interaction. In the course of the work both questions were sharpened by the structure of the task. The cyclic task is learned most effectively when the thread is simulated by a kinematic coupling that ties axial progress to rotation, when the RL teacher is responsible for the turn, and when scripted controllers provide the transitions between turns (Chapter~\ref{ch:method}). The thesis therefore refines the two questions into four that organize its experiments:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Does a single student trained on complete multi-cycle demonstrations outperform repeated execution of a single-cycle policy (Q1)?
  \item Which visual representation preserves the geometry needed for repeated re-grasping under viewpoint and axial change, a point-cloud encoder or an image-space encoder (Q2)?
  \item Does a generative flow-matching action head distill the heterogeneous demonstrations more effectively than deterministic regression with recurrent or Transformer backbones (Q3)?
  \item Does a student trained on simulated demonstrations only transfer zero-shot to the physical robot when the camera model is calibrated against the physical cell, the visual representation is metric, and the rendered appearance is randomized (Q4)?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions}\label{sec:contributions}

The thesis makes the following contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A formulation of long-horizon cyclic nut threading as an ordered sequence of phases with explicit cyclic and episodic tracking errors, and a hybrid teacher that combines a privileged RL policy for approach, grasp, rotation, and goal reaching with scripted controllers for release and return. The hybrid teacher generates coherent multi-cycle demonstrations with physically gated phase boundaries, and every phase is specified by the mechanism that specifies it most simply.
  \item A zero-shot sim-to-real recipe for the visuomotor student: the calibrated extrinsics and intrinsics of the physical camera are reproduced in simulation, the student encodes a metric point cloud cropped to the workspace, and the demonstrations are rendered under lighting, texture, and background randomization and augmented with image-space color perturbations, so that a student trained on simulated data alone acts on the physical camera stream.
  \item A unified visuomotor student distilled by conditional flow matching from point-cloud features of an aligned RGB-D view, proprioception, a global trajectory-progress scalar, and a process-phase encoding. The thesis derives the objective, describes the student as implemented, and compares it in a systematic ablation study against online DAgger variants, deterministic regression heads, alternative vision encoders, privileged observations, and a joint-space action contract.
  \item An experimental evaluation in simulation and on hardware. In simulation the student completes every ten-cycle trial on the M24 hexagonal nut with a mean cyclic tracking error of $-1.77\degree$. The hardware evaluation covers the M24, M30, and M36 hexagonal nuts it was trained on and held-out octagonal, rectangular, and round profiles; its results are \tbd.
\end{enumerate}

The work continues the author's semester thesis~\cite{Oertel2026}, which distilled a privileged grasping teacher into an RGB student with DAgger on the same hand--arm family, and it was carried out together with the advisor, who developed substantial parts of the hybrid teacher, the distillation stack, and the hardware experiments; the acknowledgments state this attribution.
